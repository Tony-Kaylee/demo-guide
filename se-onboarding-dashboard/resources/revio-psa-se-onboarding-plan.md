# Rev.io PSA Solutions Engineer Onboarding Plan

Last updated: 2026-06-10

## Purpose

This plan is designed to get a new Solutions Engineer from product orientation to confident, flexible Rev.io PSA demo delivery. The goal is not memorization. The goal is demo fluency: the SE should be able to run the core end-to-end story, explain why each step matters, and branch into deeper product areas based on prospect pain.

## Target Outcome

By the end of onboarding, the SE can:

- Deliver a full quote-to-cash demo without notes.
- Explain the operating model across CRM, quoting, service, inventory, scheduling, field work, billing, portal, analytics, automation, and integrations.
- Tailor the same demo story for telecom, MSP, security integrator, AV, and field service prospects.
- Handle common discovery pivots: inventory-heavy, project-heavy, billing-heavy, service-heavy, integration-heavy, and executive reporting-heavy.
- Use Revii moments intentionally instead of treating AI as a novelty.
- State current product strengths, known limits, and roadmap-sensitive areas accurately.

## Recommended Timeline

The plan assumes a 60-day ramp to approval for leading demos. The new SE should be shadow-ready by the end of week 2, meaning they can sit in on live demos, understand the story, take useful notes, and begin learning from real prospect interactions. They should not lead their own demos until they pass the 60-day approval gate.

The first 30 days are about product foundation, shadowing, and controlled demo execution. The second 30 days are about depth, buyer-specific branching, objection handling, and certification. A faster ramp can compress the observation and practice blocks, but the certification gates should stay intact.

### Days 1-7 - Foundation and Product Map

Focus:
- Understand what Rev.io PSA is and how the modules fit together.
- Watch or review the existing 14-step demo guide.
- Build a module map from the left navigation.
- Learn the language of the major records: customer, opportunity, quote, ticket, project, asset, product, invoice, bill profile, agreement, service.

Required outcomes:
- Can explain the platform in under two minutes.
- Can name the core modules and their business purpose.
- Can explain what each module owns in the customer lifecycle.

Core product areas:
- Home and Revii
- Customers and customer profile
- Opportunities and pipeline
- Quotes and proposal templates
- Service tickets and ticket lifecycle
- Dispatch and field tech workflow
- Billing handoff
- Customer portal

Practice exercise:
- Build a one-page module map.
- Explain each module without logging into the product.

Gate:
- Manager or mentor confirms the SE understands the product map and vocabulary.

### Days 8-14 - Shadow-Ready Observation Gate

Focus:
- Learn the end-to-end demo story.
- Explain why the connected workflow matters.
- Understand the handoffs between sales, service, dispatch, inventory, field work, billing, and the customer portal.
- Become ready to sit in on Tony's demos as an observer and active learner.

Required outcomes:
- Can narrate the quote-to-cash flow at a whiteboard level.
- Can identify the best starting point for different prospect types.
- Can explain what happens after a quote is accepted.
- Can explain why Rev.io reduces re-keying between sales, operations, and billing.
- Can shadow live demos with a structured observation checklist.
- Understands they are observing and learning, not leading demos yet.

Practice exercise:
- Give a 10-minute verbal walkthrough without logging into the product.
- Explain the same story for a telecom prospect and a security integrator prospect.
- Shadow at least one live or recorded demo and write down the prospect pain, modules shown, and follow-up items.

Gate:
- Manager or mentor confirms the SE is shadow-ready for live demos by the end of week 2.

### Days 15-21 - Guided Click Path

Focus:
- Learn the click path and timing for the primary demo.
- Learn what to avoid clicking in the demo tenant.
- Understand the safe records and recovery path if the demo changes.

Required outcomes:
- Can follow the full demo with notes.
- Can recover if a record is not where expected.
- Knows which billing and invoice actions should be narrated instead of executed.

Core demo path:
1. Dashboard and executive view
2. Customer list and global search
3. Customer profile and Revii customer summary
4. Opportunity funnel
5. Opportunity detail and attached quote
6. Quote walkthrough
7. Quote acceptance to ticket creation
8. Ticket detail, SLA, activity, and job costing
9. Revii note cleanup and activity assistance
10. Parts, labor, inventory reservation, and purchasing
11. Dispatch board and scheduling
12. Tech workflow, timer, and ready-for-billing status
13. Billing review and invoice handoff
14. Customer portal and self-service

Practice exercise:
- Run the full demo once with a mentor.
- Run the full demo once solo with notes.

Gate:
- SE can complete the demo without breaking the story or clicking destructive actions.

### Days 22-30 - Core Demo Repetition

Focus:
- Practice the standard demo until it is smooth.
- Use Revii prompts naturally during customer, ticket, inventory, and analytics moments.
- Explain the difference between recurring and one-time revenue flows.

Required outcomes:
- Can deliver the 30-45 minute full demo.
- Can deliver a 15-minute compressed version.
- Can explain transitions without sounding scripted.

Practice exercise:
- Run the full demo twice: once from notes, once without notes.
- Record a 15-minute compressed version for review.

Gate:
- SE can run the full demo cleanly and receive targeted feedback on pace, product accuracy, and story clarity.

### Days 31-38 - Operational Deep Dives

Focus:
- Build depth in operational product areas most likely to determine fit.
- Connect feature detail to buyer pain.

Primary tracks:
- Service and Ticketing
- Scheduling, Dispatch, and Mobile Field Work
- Inventory, Products, Packages, and Purchasing
- Project Management
- Billing and Revenue Operations

Practice exercise:
- Pick one operational pain prompt and run a 10-minute branch from the core demo.

Gate:
- SE can go deep on at least two operational areas without losing the platform story.

### Days 39-46 - Executive and Technical Depth

Focus:
- Build depth in executive, reporting, automation, integration, and admin conversations.

Primary tracks:
- Analytics, Revii, and Reporting
- Automation, Flows, Notifications, and Macros
- Integrations, API, and Admin

Practice exercise:
- Answer an executive reporting prompt.
- Answer a technical integration prompt.
- Explain API/custom integration positioning without overcommitting.

Gate:
- SE can separate packaged functionality from API-scoped or roadmap-sensitive work.

### Days 47-54 - Buyer Scenarios and Discovery Branching

Focus:
- Learn how to branch based on prospect pain.
- Learn proof points, language, and limitations for each feature area.
- Practice vertical-specific demo emphasis for telecom, MSP, security integrator, and AV/field service prospects.

Practice exercise:
- Run two buyer scenarios.
- Handle five objection questions.
- Write one RFP-style answer.

Gate:
- SE can choose the right deep dive based on a discovery prompt and speak for 10 minutes without losing the core story.

### Days 55-60 - Certification and Lead-Demo Approval

Focus:
- Prove demo readiness.
- Identify where the SE still needs support before fully owning live calls.

Certification requirements:
- 15-minute compressed demo
- 45-minute full demo
- One assigned deep dive
- One surprise discovery branch
- Five objection-handling questions
- One written RFP-style answer

Certification rubric:
- Story clarity
- Product accuracy
- Click-path confidence
- Discovery alignment
- Business value articulation
- Accurate limit/roadmap positioning
- Recovery when the demo changes

Gate:
- Manager or mentor approves the SE to lead demos or provides specific coaching notes before approval.

## Deep Dive Tracks

The deep dive tracks below support the second half of the 60-day ramp.

#### Customer Management and CRM

What to master:
- Customer list, tags, filters, parent/child hierarchy
- Customer profile layout
- Contacts, addresses, notes, files, services, agreements, assets
- Customer portal users and permissions
- Global search and customer profile ticket search

SE should be able to answer:
- How do customers, contacts, bill profiles, and service addresses relate?
- Can Rev.io support parent/child account structures?
- How does Rev.io keep account context visible across sales, service, and billing?

Demo exercise:
- Find a customer, explain their profile, show history, tickets, assets, quotes, and billing context.

#### Sales, Quoting, and Commerce

What to master:
- Opportunity list, funnel stages, forecast context
- Quote builder build/design tabs
- Proposal templates, variables, signatures, fillable fields
- One-time vs recurring quote lines
- Packages, product quantities, product images, and quote presentation

SE should be able to answer:
- How does an opportunity become an operational workflow?
- How do quote lines flow to tickets, recurring services, and billing?
- How does Rev.io handle complex quotes with sections, hardware, labor, and recurring revenue?

Demo exercise:
- Walk through a quote and explain what each line item becomes downstream.

#### Service and Ticketing

What to master:
- Ticket list, status, type, priority, severity, SLA
- Work requested, activity feed, internal/public notes
- Macros, recurring tickets, task creation
- Ticket stage panel, time tracking, transaction details, job costing
- Ready for Billing workflow

SE should be able to answer:
- How does Rev.io manage break/fix, installation, onboarding, project, and recurring work?
- How do techs log time and parts?
- How does the billing team know what is ready?

Demo exercise:
- Open a ticket, explain the entire lifecycle, add a safe internal note, and narrate the billing handoff.

#### Scheduling, Dispatch, and Mobile Field Work

What to master:
- Dispatch board
- Calendar views
- Unscheduled ticket queue
- Tech assignment
- Mobile workflow, timer, SLA visibility, internal emails
- GPS, geofencing, auto arrival/departure notes, travel timer

SE should be able to answer:
- How does dispatch assign work?
- What does the tech do from the field?
- What is visible on mobile vs desktop?

Demo exercise:
- Schedule a ticket from the dispatch board and explain what changes for the technician.

#### Inventory, Products, Packages, and Purchasing

What to master:
- Product catalog
- Product images, categories, SKU/USOC, tax class, GL code
- Product configuration toggles
- Packages and package quantities
- Inventory matrix, locations, reservations, transfers, serial lookup
- Purchase orders and providers
- Ticket parts/labor descriptions carrying through to billing

SE should be able to answer:
- Can Rev.io track truck stock and warehouse inventory?
- Can serialized equipment be tracked across movement history?

Demo exercise:
- Show product catalog, reserve inventory from a ticket, and explain serialized movement history.

#### Project Management

What to master:

- Linked tickets and tasks

Demo exercise:
- Show a project, explain phases/milestones, link the operational work to tickets/tasks, and show project cost visibility.

- Project Management should be positioned around current available functionality and active investment. Avoid overstating quote-to-project automation unless confirmed for the specific workflow.

## Standard End-to-End Demo Talk Track

The core narrative:

Rev.io connects the customer lifecycle from opportunity to quote, quote to operational work, operational work to billing, and billing to customer self-service. The value is not just that each module exists. The value is that the handoffs stay inside one operating system.

Primary flow:
- Start with executive visibility.
- Show how a customer is found and understood.
- Move into opportunity and quote.
- Show how accepted work becomes a ticket.
- Show how service teams schedule, perform, document, and cost the work.
- Show how completed work becomes billable.
- Close with the customer portal and analytics.

Core line:

"The important part is that sales, service, dispatch, inventory, field work, and billing are not separate stories. They are one connected workflow."

## Revii Usage Guide

Use Revii when it increases credibility or speed. Do not overuse it.

Best demo moments:
- Customer activity summary before a call
- Open ticket count or recent ticket summary
- Create a ticket from natural language
- Add an internal activity note
- Reformat technician notes with Rev.iize
- Log time conversationally
- Ask analytics questions in Chat With Your Data

Avoid:
- Making Revii the whole demo
- Using AI where a normal workflow is clearer
- Asking untested prompts in a live prospect demo

## Known Positioning Notes

- Project Management is strong for project visibility, phases, milestones, Gantt, linked work, files, and job costing. Be careful with automatic quote-to-project claims unless confirmed for the current release/workflow.
- Product catalog images are available as of the 2026-05-28 release.
- IT Glue and Hudu integrations are available in the 2026-06-02 release context.
- NetSuite should be positioned as a custom integration/API-scoped effort unless a packaged connector is confirmed.
- Do not create live invoices during demo unless the environment is explicitly prepared for it.
- Be clear about PSA vs legacy Billing boundaries when discussing recurring services and order-based activation.

## Final Capstone

The new SE receives a discovery prompt and must deliver:

1. A three-minute demo strategy.
2. A full demo with one deep dive.
3. A short answer to "Why Rev.io instead of the tool we use today?"
4. A follow-up plan with what should be validated after the call.

Passing standard:

The SE sounds like they understand how the prospect runs their business, not just where buttons are in the product.
